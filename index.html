How to get MANLY

Open the camera or upload a photo. Pick a plaster. In Photo mode, tap the shutter to freeze and fine-tune. In Video mode, tap to record and tap again to stop.

Your face stays on your device.

Photos, camera frames, audio, and face landmarks are processed in your browser. MANLYFY does not upload them. There are no accounts, ads, analytics, or subscriptions. Your browser downloads the app and face-tracking model from this website. Only your design and display preferences may be stored locally.

Camera & microphone

The camera needs HTTPS and browser permission. The microphone is optional and off by default; turn it on in Video mode before recording. Denying the mic still allows silent video. Camera and mic stop when you leave the tab or turn the camera off.

Nose not cooperating?

Face forward in even light, with your whole face visible. Group mode finds up to 8 faces in photos and 4 live. Small, covered, dark, or strongly sideways faces may be missed. For photos, add and drag a plaster manually. For slower phones choose Lite mode.

Downloads & sharing

Photos download as watermark-free PNG files, up to 2560 pixels. 9:16 and square formats fit your whole photo with a dark border. Videos record at up to 1280 pixels and download as WebM or MP4 according to your browser. Some social apps need a format conversion. Native file sharing appears where supported; otherwise download and share from your gallery.

Install & offline

On Android Chrome, tap Install app or use the browser menu → Add to Home screen. On iPhone Safari, use Share → Add to Home Screen. After the tracker has loaded once online, previously loaded app and tracking files can be reused offline. Browser storage can be cleared or evicted.

A few honest limits

Recording requires MediaRecorder and canvas capture support. Record with the tab visible; switching away stops the recording. Clips stop at 3 minutes to protect phone memory. Tracking is a playful 2D effect, not a perfect 3D wrap. Browsers without WebGL2 use a slower local CPU tracker. Real Android devices and low-powered hardware still need hands-on testing.
